---
name: FIAREP organization development catalog
description: Authority rules for the development names available to each organization and its staff.
---

Platform Control establishes each organization's approved development-name list. Human Resources may extend its own organization's list from an official catalog or reviewed CSV and assign those configured names to employees. Organize this control data as Organization → Organization Type → Portfolio → Developments. NYCHA organizations may load the shared NYCHA catalog; other organizations use the names supplied during onboarding. Staff assignment must use this organization list rather than discovering or inventing names from staff records.

A configured development name and a registered property are separate records. Platform Control should show the registered property addresses grouped beneath their matching development so an empty property set is visible rather than implied.

Development-level operational scoring follows the authorized development assignment on each record and aggregates every address under that development. A missing registered-property row must not remove valid work from the development score.

A resident complaint must have an authorized development before staff can complete or resolve it. When the complaint is missing one and the acting staff member has exactly one assigned development, that assignment may be applied automatically; otherwise completion must fail until a development is selected.

Built-in organization catalogs are exclusive to the organization they name. Loading one replaces that organization’s known catalog instead of merging it, and unrelated organizations must not see catalog controls for NYCHA or L+M.

Unknown organization catalogs may be researched with AI, but results must remain review-first, include public source links, prefer the official organization website, and never infer development counts from unit counts.

**Why:** Discovering developments from existing assignments creates a circular dependency: a new employee cannot be assigned until a development already appears on another record. HR needs an operational path to add reviewed names and assign them without waiting for Platform Control. A portfolio label alone does not establish property ownership or provide addresses. A valid Jefferson report was excluded from scoring when its development had no registered address row, even though the report's authorized development assignment was correct. Allowing complaints to finish without a development also prevents reliable development aggregation. Cross-organization catalog controls allowed NYCHA and L+M names to be combined into an invalid portfolio. Public web results can be incomplete or ambiguous, so researched entries must not become authoritative without review.

**How to apply:** Save organization type and development names with the organization, show the resulting portfolio size in Platform Control, and group registered addresses by exact normalized development name. Let HR add names only to its authenticated tenant through reviewed CSV or matching official catalog search, then assign configured names to eligible employees. Match built-in catalogs by normalized organization identity, expose only the matching catalog control, and replace rather than merge authoritative lists when Platform Control reloads the baseline. For unknown organizations, show research sources and let Platform Control remove incorrect entries before saving. Expose only approved names to tenant staff, and require non-Borough-Director staff accounts to receive at least one approved development assignment. For authorized operational records, calculate the development aggregate from the record's development assignment; use property rows for address registration and display, not as a prerequisite for scoring. On complaint completion, persist the development on both the record and its workflow state.
